Research Stack Add-On: P/E, EPS Overlay, PEG Analysis, and Sector Peer Position Grade
====================================================================================

Use this module to expand your existing fundamental-analysis prompt stack. It is designed to help you supplement technical analysis by checking whether the stock's price action is supported by earnings, valuation, growth-adjusted valuation, and relative strength versus relevant sector peers.

Placeholders
------------

{TICKER} = stock ticker
{COMPANY} = company name
{SECTOR} = sector, if known
{INDUSTRY} = industry or subsector, if known
{PEER_TICKERS} = peer tickers, if you already know them
{AS_OF_DATE} = current date
{LOOKBACK_PERIOD} = e.g. 3 years, 5 years, 10 years
{TIMEFRAME} = trading/investing timeframe, e.g. swing trade, 3-6 months, 1-3 years
{TECHNICAL_SETUP} = your technical analysis view, e.g. breakout, support retest, downtrend reversal
{RISK_TOLERANCE} = low, medium, high

Important Data Rules
--------------------

Add this clause to any of the prompts below:

Do not guess or fabricate data. Use current sources and cite all financial figures. Clearly state whether EPS is GAAP EPS, adjusted EPS, diluted EPS, trailing twelve-month EPS, or forward estimated EPS. Clearly state whether P/E is trailing P/E or forward P/E. If a company has negative earnings or negative expected growth, explain that P/E or PEG may not be meaningful and use alternative valuation metrics such as EV/Sales, EV/EBITDA, price/book, revenue growth, or free cash flow yield where appropriate.

Key Formulas
------------

Trailing P/E = Current share price / trailing twelve-month diluted EPS
Forward P/E = Current share price / next fiscal year expected EPS
TTM EPS = Sum of the last four quarters of diluted EPS
EPS growth % = ((Current EPS / Prior-period EPS) - 1) x 100
PEG ratio = Forward P/E / expected EPS growth rate percentage

Example PEG calculation:
If forward P/E is 40 and expected EPS growth is 25%, PEG = 40 / 25 = 1.6.

Interpret PEG carefully:
- PEG below 1.0 can suggest growth is cheap, but only if growth estimates are reliable.
- PEG around 1.0-2.0 can be reasonable for high-quality growth companies.
- PEG above 2.0 can suggest the market is paying aggressively for growth.
- PEG is not useful when EPS is negative, expected EPS growth is negative, or earnings are highly cyclical.


31. Price + P/E + EPS Overlay Prompt
------------------------------------

Use this prompt to create a visual overlay of price, P/E ratio, and EPS trend.

```text
Act as an equity research analyst and market data analyst.

For {COMPANY} / {TICKER}, fetch and analyze the relationship between stock price, trailing P/E ratio, forward P/E ratio, and EPS over {LOOKBACK_PERIOD}, as of {AS_OF_DATE}.

Use reliable current data sources and cite them. Prioritize:
1. Company filings and earnings releases for EPS data
2. Reputable market data sources for historical prices and valuation ratios
3. Analyst estimate sources for forward EPS and forward P/E, if available

Create a table with these columns:
- Date
- Stock price or adjusted close
- Quarterly diluted EPS
- TTM diluted EPS
- Forward EPS estimate, if available
- Trailing P/E
- Forward P/E
- YoY EPS growth
- Notes on major earnings events

Then create or describe these charts:
1. Stock price overlaid with trailing P/E ratio
   - Stock price on the left y-axis
   - Trailing P/E on the right y-axis

2. Stock price overlaid with TTM EPS
   - Stock price on the left y-axis
   - TTM EPS on the right y-axis
   - Mark major EPS inflection points

3. Optional: Forward P/E vs trailing P/E
   - Show whether the market is pricing in earnings acceleration or deceleration

Analyze:
1. Did the stock price rise because EPS rose, because the P/E multiple expanded, or both?
2. Did the stock price fall because EPS declined, because the P/E multiple compressed, or both?
3. Is the stock currently trading above or below its historical average P/E?
4. Is EPS growth confirming the price trend?
5. Are earnings improving faster or slower than the stock price?
6. Does the P/E trend support or contradict my technical setup: {TECHNICAL_SETUP}?

End with:
- Price/EPS confirmation: Strong, Moderate, Weak, or Negative
- P/E risk: Low, Moderate, High, or Extreme
- One-paragraph chart interpretation
```


32. P/E Ratio Deep-Dive Prompt
------------------------------

Use this to decide whether the stock's multiple is justified.

```text
Analyze the P/E ratio of {COMPANY} / {TICKER} as of {AS_OF_DATE}.

Include:
- Current trailing P/E
- Current forward P/E
- 3-year and 5-year average P/E, if available
- Historical P/E range
- Sector median P/E
- Peer median P/E
- EPS growth rate
- Revenue growth rate
- Margin trend
- Free cash flow trend

Answer:
1. Is the current P/E high, low, or normal compared with the company's own history?
2. Is the current P/E high, low, or normal compared with peers?
3. Is the P/E being driven by strong earnings growth or by multiple expansion?
4. Is the P/E vulnerable to compression if growth slows?
5. Does the current P/E make sense given revenue growth, EPS growth, margins, and balance sheet quality?
6. Would the stock still look attractive if the P/E reverted to its historical average?

Create a small valuation sensitivity table showing estimated stock price under different P/E multiples:
- Bear-case P/E
- Base-case P/E
- Bull-case P/E
- Current P/E

Use current or forward EPS assumptions and clearly state them.

End with:
- P/E verdict: Cheap, Reasonable, Expensive, Very Expensive, or Not Meaningful
- P/E risk score from 1 to 10
- Whether P/E supports, weakens, or is neutral to my technical setup: {TECHNICAL_SETUP}
```


33. EPS Trend and Earnings Power Prompt
---------------------------------------

Use this to determine whether price action is supported by earnings growth.

```text
Analyze the EPS trend and earnings power of {COMPANY} / {TICKER}.

Fetch:
- Quarterly GAAP EPS for the last 8 quarters
- Quarterly adjusted EPS for the last 8 quarters, if available
- TTM diluted EPS trend
- Forward EPS estimates for the next 1-3 fiscal years, if available
- EPS revisions over the last 30, 60, and 90 days, if available
- Earnings beat/miss history

Create a table with:
- Quarter
- Reported EPS
- Adjusted EPS
- Analyst estimate
- Beat or miss
- YoY EPS growth
- Sequential EPS growth
- Management guidance commentary

Analyze:
1. Is EPS accelerating, decelerating, stable, or declining?
2. Are EPS gains coming from revenue growth, margin expansion, buybacks, cost cuts, or one-time items?
3. Are analyst EPS estimates being revised upward or downward?
4. Is EPS quality high or low?
5. Is the market paying more for each dollar of earnings than it did historically?
6. Does EPS momentum confirm my chart setup: {TECHNICAL_SETUP}?

End with:
- EPS momentum rating: Strong, Positive, Mixed, Weak, or Negative
- Earnings quality rating: Strong, Good, Mixed, Weak, or Poor
- Key EPS number to monitor next quarter
```


34. PEG Analysis Prompt
-----------------------

Use this to evaluate whether the P/E multiple is reasonable relative to growth.

```text
Perform a PEG analysis for {COMPANY} / {TICKER} as of {AS_OF_DATE}.

Use current data and cite sources. Fetch:
- Current stock price
- Trailing P/E
- Forward P/E
- Expected EPS growth for next fiscal year
- Expected EPS CAGR over the next 3-5 years, if available
- Historical EPS growth over the last 3-5 years
- Revenue growth
- Free cash flow growth
- Peer PEG ratios
- Sector median PEG ratio

Calculate:
1. One-year forward PEG = Forward P/E / next-year expected EPS growth percentage
2. Multi-year PEG = Forward P/E / expected 3-5 year EPS CAGR percentage
3. Historical PEG = Current P/E / historical EPS CAGR percentage, if meaningful

Explain any limitations:
- Negative EPS
- Negative expected growth
- Unreliable analyst estimates
- Cyclical earnings
- One-time earnings effects
- Major acquisitions or divestitures

Create a PEG sensitivity table:
- Forward P/E
- EPS growth assumption
- Implied PEG
- Interpretation

Use growth assumptions such as:
- Bear case
- Base case
- Bull case
- Analyst consensus case

Answer:
1. Is the stock cheap, fair, or expensive on a growth-adjusted basis?
2. Is the market pricing in realistic growth?
3. How much EPS growth is needed to justify the current P/E?
4. Does the PEG ratio support a long trade, weaken it, or suggest caution?
5. How does the company's PEG compare with peers: {PEER_TICKERS}?

End with:
- PEG verdict: Attractive, Reasonable, Stretched, Expensive, or Not Meaningful
- Growth-adjusted valuation score from 1 to 10
- Biggest assumption behind the PEG conclusion
```


35. Relevant Sector Peer Research Prompt
----------------------------------------

Use this to compare the stock against the most relevant companies in its sector or industry.

```text
Act as a sector analyst.

For {COMPANY} / {TICKER}, identify the most relevant peer companies in the same sector, industry, and business model as of {AS_OF_DATE}.

If I provide peers, use these first: {PEER_TICKERS}.
If I do not provide peers, select 5-10 relevant peers and explain why each peer is included.

Avoid weak comparisons. Do not compare companies only because they are in the same broad sector. Prefer peers with similar:
- Products or services
- End markets
- Customer base
- Revenue model
- Growth profile
- Margin profile
- Market capitalization range, where possible
- Geographic exposure

Create a peer comparison table with:
- Company
- Ticker
- Market cap
- Revenue growth
- EPS growth
- Gross margin
- Operating margin
- Net margin
- Free cash flow margin
- Return on invested capital, if available
- Balance sheet risk
- Trailing P/E
- Forward P/E
- PEG ratio
- EV/Sales
- EV/EBITDA
- Price/free cash flow
- Dividend yield, if relevant
- Debt/EBITDA, if relevant

Then rank the companies by:
1. Growth
2. Profitability
3. Earnings quality
4. Balance sheet strength
5. Valuation
6. PEG / growth-adjusted value
7. Free cash flow quality
8. Risk profile
9. Overall fundamental quality

Answer:
1. Which peer is the best growth company?
2. Which peer is the most profitable?
3. Which peer has the strongest balance sheet?
4. Which peer has the best growth-adjusted valuation?
5. Which peer is most overvalued relative to growth?
6. Which peer has the best risk/reward?
7. Does {TICKER} deserve a premium or discount to peers?

End with:
- Sector-relative rank for {TICKER}
- Sector-relative grade: A, B, C, D, or F
- Explanation of whether the stock is a sector leader, average performer, laggard, or speculative outlier
```

37. Position Grade Prompt
-------------------------

Use this to grade whether fundamentals support the position.

```text
Create a final fundamental position grade for {COMPANY} / {TICKER} as of {AS_OF_DATE}.

My technical setup is:
{TECHNICAL_SETUP}

My timeframe is:
{TIMEFRAME}

My risk tolerance is:
{RISK_TOLERANCE}

Use the following weighted scorecard:

1. EPS trend and earnings momentum - 15 points
2. Price/EPS confirmation - 10 points
3. P/E valuation risk - 10 points
4. PEG / growth-adjusted valuation - 15 points
5. Revenue growth and margin trend - 10 points
6. Free cash flow and balance sheet quality - 10 points
7. Sector peer ranking - 15 points
8. Catalysts and event risk - 5 points
9. Red flags and downside risk - 5 points
10. Alignment with technical setup - 5 points

Total score: 100 points

Grade scale:
- A: 85-100 = Fundamentals strongly support the position
- B: 75-84 = Fundamentals support the position, but watch valuation or risk
- C: 60-74 = Mixed fundamentals; position depends heavily on timing and risk control
- D: 45-59 = Weak fundamental support; mostly technical or speculative
- F: Below 45 = Fundamentals do not support the position

For each category, provide:
- Score
- Evidence
- Bullish interpretation
- Bearish interpretation
- Data confidence level

Then answer:
1. Is this a fundamentally supported technical setup?
2. Is the current price move earnings-driven or multiple-driven?
3. Is valuation reasonable relative to growth?
4. Is the company better or worse than relevant peers?
5. What would invalidate the position fundamentally?
6. What should I monitor next earnings report?

End with:
- Final score out of 100
- Final grade: A, B, C, D, or F
- Position classification:
  1. Fundamentally supported position
  2. Fundamentally supported but valuation-sensitive
  3. Mixed fundamentals / technical trade only
  4. Speculative high-risk position
  5. Avoid due to weak fundamentals
- One-paragraph position summary
```


38. All-in-One Research Stack Prompt With P/E, EPS, PEG, and Peers
------------------------------------------------------------------

Use this as the new master prompt when you want the full updated research stack.

```text
Act as an institutional equity research analyst and market data analyst.

Analyze {COMPANY} / {TICKER} as of {AS_OF_DATE} to determine whether the fundamentals support my technical analysis setup.

My technical setup is:
{TECHNICAL_SETUP}

My timeframe is:
{TIMEFRAME}

My risk tolerance is:
{RISK_TOLERANCE}

Use current and reliable sources. Prioritize SEC filings, company earnings releases, investor presentations, earnings call transcripts, reputable market data providers, and analyst estimate sources where available. Cite all important figures.

Important:
- Do not fabricate numbers.
- Clearly label GAAP EPS, adjusted EPS, diluted EPS, TTM EPS, and forward EPS.
- Clearly distinguish trailing P/E from forward P/E.
- If P/E or PEG is not meaningful, explain why.
- Use peer comparisons only against relevant companies.

Analyze the company across these sections:

1. Business overview
   - What the company does
   - Main revenue drivers
   - Segment exposure
   - Geographic exposure
   - Key customers or end markets

2. Stock price, P/E, and EPS overlay
   - Fetch stock price history over {LOOKBACK_PERIOD}
   - Fetch quarterly EPS and TTM EPS over the same period
   - Fetch trailing P/E and forward P/E
   - Explain whether price movement is driven by EPS growth, P/E expansion, or both
   - Describe or create a chart overlaying stock price, P/E, and EPS

3. EPS trend and earnings quality
   - Quarterly EPS trend
   - TTM EPS trend
   - EPS growth
   - Beat/miss history
   - EPS revisions
   - Quality of earnings

4. P/E valuation analysis
   - Current trailing P/E
   - Current forward P/E
   - Historical P/E range
   - Peer median P/E
   - Sector median P/E
   - Valuation risk if the multiple compresses

5. PEG analysis
   - Forward P/E
   - Expected EPS growth
   - 3-5 year EPS CAGR estimate, if available
   - PEG ratio
   - PEG compared with peers
   - Sensitivity table using bear/base/bull growth assumptions

6. Revenue, margins, and cash flow
   - Revenue growth
   - Gross margin
   - Operating margin
   - Net margin
   - Free cash flow
   - Free cash flow margin
   - Balance sheet health

7. Sector and peer comparison
   - Identify the most relevant peers
   - Explain why each peer is relevant
   - Compare growth, margins, EPS growth, P/E, PEG, EV/Sales, EV/EBITDA, free cash flow, debt, and sector-specific metrics
   - Rank the company versus peers

8. Catalysts and risks
   - Upcoming earnings
   - Guidance updates
   - Product launches
   - Regulatory or legal events
   - Macro or sector risks
   - Red flags

9. Technical setup confirmation
   - Explain whether fundamentals confirm, partially confirm, are neutral to, or contradict the technical setup
   - Identify event risks that could break the chart setup
   - Identify the most important fundamental invalidation point

10. Final position grade
   Use this 100-point scorecard:
   - EPS trend and earnings momentum: 15
   - Price/EPS confirmation: 10
   - P/E valuation risk: 10
   - PEG / growth-adjusted valuation: 15
   - Revenue growth and margin trend: 10
   - Free cash flow and balance sheet quality: 10
   - Sector peer ranking: 15
   - Catalysts and event risk: 5
   - Red flags and downside risk: 5
   - Alignment with technical setup: 5

End with:
- Final score out of 100
- Final grade: A, B, C, D, or F
- Sector-relative rank
- Strongest bullish evidence
- Strongest bearish evidence
- Current valuation risk
- Key EPS metric to monitor
- Key peer comparison metric to monitor
- Final classification:
  1. Fundamentally supported position
  2. Fundamentally supported but valuation-sensitive
  3. Mixed fundamentals / technical trade only
  4. Speculative high-risk position
  5. Avoid due to weak fundamentals

Do not give a guaranteed buy or sell recommendation. Give a research-based position grade that helps me decide whether the chart setup has fundamental support.
```

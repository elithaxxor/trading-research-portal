Fundamental Analysis Prompt Suite for Supplementing Technical Analysis

Use these prompts with a web-enabled AI research tool, market data platform, or finance-focused agent. Replace the placeholders before running each prompt.

Placeholders

{TICKER} = stock ticker
{COMPANY} = company name
{EXCHANGE} = exchange, if known
{AS_OF_DATE} = today’s date
{TIMEFRAME} = trading/investing timeframe, e.g. swing trade, 3–6 months, 1–3 years
{PEERS} = direct competitors or peer tickers
{TECHNICAL_SETUP} = your technical analysis view, e.g. breakout, support retest, downtrend reversal
{RISK_TOLERANCE} = low, medium, high

============================================================
1. MASTER FUNDAMENTAL ANALYSIS PROMPT
============================================================

Use this when you want a full fundamental report.

Act as an institutional equity research analyst.

I want a fundamental analysis of {COMPANY} / {TICKER} listed on {EXCHANGE}, as of {AS_OF_DATE}. The purpose is to supplement my technical analysis, not replace it.

Use current and reliable sources, prioritizing:
1. Latest annual report / 10-K
2. Latest quarterly report / 10-Q
3. Latest earnings release
4. Latest investor presentation
5. Earnings call transcript
6. Company investor relations website
7. Reputable financial data sources
8. Industry or macro data where relevant

Important rules:
- Cite every major financial figure.
- Clearly separate reported facts from your interpretation.
- Do not invent numbers.
- If a metric is unavailable, say so.
- Use the latest available data.
- Compare recent results against prior periods.
- Highlight both bullish and bearish evidence.

Analyze the company across these sections:

1. Business overview
   - What the company does
   - Main products/services
   - Revenue segments
   - Geographic exposure
   - Customer concentration, if available

2. Revenue and growth
   - Revenue trend over the last 3–5 years
   - Latest quarterly revenue growth
   - Organic vs acquisition-driven growth, if available
   - Growth consistency
   - Key growth drivers

3. Profitability
   - Gross margin
   - Operating margin
   - Net margin
   - EBITDA margin, if relevant
   - Margin trend and explanation

4. Balance sheet strength
   - Cash
   - Debt
   - Net debt
   - Current ratio
   - Debt-to-equity
   - Interest coverage, if available
   - Liquidity risk

5. Cash flow quality
   - Operating cash flow
   - Free cash flow
   - Capital expenditures
   - Free cash flow margin
   - Cash conversion quality
   - Whether earnings are backed by cash flow

6. Valuation
   - Market cap
   - Enterprise value
   - P/E
   - Forward P/E
   - EV/EBITDA
   - EV/Sales
   - Price/free cash flow
   - Compare valuation to historical average and peers

7. Competitive position
   - Moat
   - Pricing power
   - Brand strength
   - Switching costs
   - Scale advantages
   - Network effects, if applicable
   - Threats from competitors

8. Management and capital allocation
   - CEO/CFO background
   - Insider ownership
   - Share buybacks
   - Dividends
   - Acquisitions
   - Share dilution
   - Debt management

9. Risks
   - Business risks
   - Financial risks
   - Regulatory risks
   - Macro risks
   - Customer/product concentration
   - Execution risks

10. Catalysts
   - Upcoming earnings
   - Product launches
   - Regulatory decisions
   - Guidance updates
   - Investor day
   - Buybacks/dividends
   - Industry events

11. Fundamental scorecard
   Score each category from 1 to 10:
   - Growth
   - Profitability
   - Balance sheet
   - Cash flow
   - Valuation
   - Competitive advantage
   - Management quality
   - Risk profile

12. Technical analysis integration
   Given my technical setup is: {TECHNICAL_SETUP}
   Explain whether the fundamentals:
   - Confirm the technical setup
   - Contradict the technical setup
   - Are neutral
   - Suggest this is better suited for a short-term trade, medium-term swing, or long-term investment

End with:
- Bull case
- Bear case
- Base case
- Key numbers to monitor next quarter
- Final fundamental rating: Strong, Good, Mixed, Weak, or Avoid

Use reasonable assumptions for:
- Revenue base
- Free cash flow margin
- Discount rate
- Terminal growth rate
- Share count
- Net debt or net cash

Show:
1. Current market-implied assumptions
2. Conservative scenario
3. Base scenario
4. Optimistic scenario
5. What has to go right for the current valuation to make sense
6. What could cause downside if expectations are too high

End with:
- “Priced for perfection”
- “Priced for solid execution”
- “Priced conservatively”
- or “Unclear due to insufficient data”

Clearly state all assumptions.

17. FUNDAMENTAL + TECHNICAL CONFIRMATION PROMPT
============================================================

This is one of the most useful prompts for your specific goal.

I have the following technical analysis setup for {COMPANY} / {TICKER}:

{TECHNICAL_SETUP}

Now analyze whether the fundamentals support this setup.

Check:
- Latest earnings trend
- Revenue growth
- Margin direction
- Free cash flow
- Balance sheet
- Valuation
- Guidance
- Catalysts
- Institutional sentiment, if available
- Insider activity, if available
- Peer performance
- Sector trend

Answer in this format:

1. Fundamental confirmation
   - What supports the technical setup?

2. Fundamental contradiction
   - What weakens or contradicts the technical setup?

3. Timing risk
   - Are there upcoming events that could invalidate the chart?

4. Trade quality
   - Does this look like a technical-only trade, a fundamentally supported trade, or a potential investment?

5. Risk level
   - Low, medium, or high

6. Final view
   - Fundamentals confirm, partially confirm, are neutral, or contradict the technical setup.

Do not give a guaranteed buy or sell recommendation. Give a research-based assessment.

Research Stack Add-On: P/E, EPS Overlay, PEG Analysis, and Sector Peer Position Grade
====================================================================================

Use this module to expand your existing fundamental-analysis prompt stack. It is designed to help you supplement technical analysis by checking whether the stock's price action is supported by earnings, valuation, growth-adjusted valuation, and relative strength versus relevant sector peers.

Placeholders
------------

{TICKER} = stock ticker
{COMPANY} = company name
{SECTOR} = sector, if known
{INDUSTRY} = industry or subsector, if known
{PEER_TICKERS} = peer tickers, if you already know them

{TIMEFRAME} = trading/investing timeframe, e.g. swing trade, 3-6 months, 1-3 years
{TECHNICAL_SETUP} = your technical analysis view, e.g. breakout, support retest, downtrend reversal
{RISK_TOLERANCE} = low, medium, high

Important Data Rules
--------------------

Add this clause to any of the prompts below:

Do not guess or fabricate data. Use current sources and cite all financial figures. Clearly state whether EPS is GAAP EPS, adjusted EPS, diluted EPS, trailing twelve-month EPS, or forward estimated EPS. Clearly state whether P/E is trailing P/E or forward P/E. If a company has negative earnings or negative expected growth, explain that P/E or PEG may not be meaningful and use alternative valuation metrics such as EV/Sales, EV/EBITDA, price/book, revenue growth, or free cash flow yield where appropriate.

Key Formulas
------------

Trailing P/E = Current share price / trailing twelve-month diluted EPS
Forward P/E = Current share price / next fiscal year expected EPS
TTM EPS = Sum of the last four quarters of diluted EPS
EPS growth % = ((Current EPS / Prior-period EPS) - 1) x 100
PEG ratio = Forward P/E / expected EPS growth rate percentage

Example PEG calculation:
If forward P/E is 40 and expected EPS growth is 25%, PEG = 40 / 25 = 1.6.

Interpret PEG carefully:
- PEG below 1.0 can suggest growth is cheap, but only if growth estimates are reliable.
- PEG around 1.0-2.0 can be reasonable for high-quality growth companies.
- PEG above 2.0 can suggest the market is paying aggressively for growth.
- PEG is not useful when EPS is negative, expected EPS growth is negative, or earnings are highly cyclical.
